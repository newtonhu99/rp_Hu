Effect of different concentrations of erythritol on regeneration and survival of Girardia tigrina

Research Project conducted at HTHS

Background

Erythritol is a common sugar substitute that is used in many sweet foods. However, over past years of research, possible health concerns have been raised about the safety of this chemical. An increase in programmed cell death was observed, and an increase of oxidative stress (the production of Reactive Oxygen Species) was also observed in cells exposed in erythritol. These results raise massive concerns about the possible effects of erythritol on human cell health.

Rationale

Results from this study could very much contribute to the information known about erythritol right now. If the effects of erythritol on planaria growth is understood more because of this study, it can possibly help the general public understand more about this substance and the possible side effects on cell health of ingesting it. Planaria are a great model organism for human stem cells. This could help prompt further research about the substance, and possibly prompt researchers to possibly improve upon it or find a replacement if any drastic negative effects are observed. If further research does continue on this topic, then normal people who use erythritol as their main sweetener will be benefited, as they can be more aware of its effects. People with young children could also be benefited, as children have the most stem cell development, so if negative results come out, these people will know.

Hypothesis

This study hypothesized that harmful changes and effects will happen to the stem cells when planaria of species G. tigrina was placed into water with erythritol, causing decreased growth, and that the effects will increase in scope as the concentration increases. The objective of this study was to determine whether erythritol would have a detrimental effect on stem cells and therefore affect their growth rate and possibly cause death.
